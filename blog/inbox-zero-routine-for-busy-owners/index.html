Inbox Zero: My Routine for Busy Business Owners

November 24, 2025 (11mo ago) · 2 min read

Most owners I meet do not have an email problem. They have a sorting problem: hundreds of unread messages, and no way to tell which three actually matter today. Inbox zero, the way I practice it, means every message has been seen, sorted and given a next step — not that the inbox is magically empty forever.

Step 1: Sort before you answer

I start by scanning, not replying. Messages get labels or flags: Needs you today, I can answer this, Waiting on someone else, and File it. Only after the sort do I open anything in full. Answering as you read is how two hours disappear.

Step 2: Templates for the repeat questions

Every business gets the same twenty questions. I keep warm, personal-feeling templates for those — pricing, booking, opening hours, delivery — and I customise the first and last line of each reply. The customer feels answered; the owner never typed it.

Step 3: A weekly tidy

Once a week I clear the "waiting" folder: follow up on quiet threads, archive what is finished, and send the owner a short list of anything stuck. Ten minutes weekly beats a monthly rescue mission.

What the owner actually does

Decisions. That is all that reaches them — a short, clear list of what needs their judgment. Everything else has already been handled.
